I am a cloud engineer and architect with five years of experience building and operating the layer beneath the product.

My work sits where the abstractions meet the hardware: Linux hosts, OpenStack control planes, Ceph storage clusters, and Kubernetes platforms. I like systems that are understandable under pressure — clear failure domains, honest dashboards, boring upgrades, and runbooks that do not assume the person reading them wrote the system.

## What I work on

- **Private cloud architecture.** OpenStack control planes, tenant networking, quotas, and lifecycle management.
- **Distributed storage.** Ceph placement groups, failure domains, recovery behaviour, and the performance characteristics that show up in production.
- **Kubernetes platforms.** Cluster design, controllers, scheduling, storage integration, and the operational surface around them.
- **Linux and automation.** Performance investigation, observability, packaging, and the small tools that remove repeated work.

## How I think about platforms

Infrastructure is a product with users. The job is not to make the diagram look impressive; it is to make the next incident shorter, the next upgrade safer, and the next engineer able to reason about the system without asking me.

I write about the parts that are hard to learn from documentation alone: the seams between components, the assumptions that fail at 3 a.m., and the design choices that make operations calmer.
